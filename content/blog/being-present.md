There's a difference between being in the room and being present in it. I've been in plenty of rooms — meetings, dinners, conversations — where I was there physically and somewhere else entirely in my head. Running through the list, calculating the next move, waiting for my turn to speak rather than actually listening. It took me longer than I'd like to admit to realize how often I was doing this.

Presence is one of those things that sounds obvious until you try to practice it deliberately. The phone is the easy culprit, and yes, the phone is a problem — but it's a symptom. The deeper issue is the habit of treating the current moment as a waiting room for the next one. Finishing this task so I can get to that task. Getting through this conversation to have a different one. Living in a perpetual "once I get past this" orientation.

What shifted it for me wasn't a meditation retreat or a productivity system. It was watching my kids do things — build something, explain something, figure something out — and noticing when I was actually watching versus when my body was in the chair and my mind was somewhere in my inbox. The contrast was too obvious to ignore. You can't half-pay-attention to a six-year-old and convince yourself you were there. Kids are an excellent mirror for presence because they don't pretend.

The practice I've landed on is deliberately ordinary: when I'm in a conversation, I try to treat it as the only thing happening. Not multitasking, not monitoring other inputs. Just this. It doesn't always work. But the effort of trying has changed what I notice, which is probably the point.
